THE FAITH OF THE DISCIPLES

I would like to begin with a question.

You do not need to answer; it is just a question to help me introduce the theme of this message:

Is believing in the power of Jesus Christ and knowing Jesus
enough for us to place our complete faith in Him?

At the beginning of the Gospel according to Mark, several miracles that Jesus performed are recorded, including:

Healing of the demon-possessed man;

Healing of the leper;

Healing and forgiveness of the sins of the paralytic...

The miracles specified by Mark in the opening chapters were not the only ones Jesus performed at that time.

In chapter 3, beginning in verse 7, we find the result of the many miracles that were known by the people of that region:

Mark 3:7-11 NET

7 Then Jesus went away with his disciples to the sea, and a great multitude from Galilee followed him. And from Judea, Jerusalem, Idumea, beyond the Jordan River, and around Tyre and Sidon a great multitude came to him when they heard about the things he had done. 9 Because of the crowd, he told his disciples to have a small boat ready for him so the crowd would not press toward him. 10 For he had healed many, so that all who were afflicted with diseases pressed toward him in order to touch him. 11 And whenever the unclean spirits saw him, they fell down before him and cried out, “You are the Son of God.”

As we can see in the text, a great multitude came to Jesus;

(v. 10) And because he had healed many, all who were afflicted with diseases pressed toward Him.

According to the original Scriptures, this expression “pressed toward Him,” translated from the Greek, also means something like “fell upon Him,” “threw themselves upon Him,” or “rushed upon Him”.

Try to picture this, brothers and sisters: a great multitude;

People who came from various parts of Israel and from the lands around Israel, most of whom had probably walked there; because they had heard about miracles;

And they came, not to see Him from a distance, but to throw themselves upon Him and try to touch Him, because they hoped that, simply by touching Jesus, their afflictions would be healed.

But today I would like to focus on the disciples of Jesus, who witnessed all of this.

The disciples who walked with Jesus; who followed Him.

And up to this point, in the Gospel according to Mark, there are two certainties we find about the disciples that I would like to highlight:

They knew Jesus and believed in Him.

Because if they did not believe, they would not have left everything to follow Him.

Imagine what it would have been like to follow Jesus as the disciples did.

To see all the signs and wonders that they saw;

To personally know the character of Jesus; to talk with Him and hear His voice; to eat with Him at the table; to be able to embrace Him.

The disciples’ faith must have been something very special;

But there was a day when they went through a storm that shook them, just as all of us may go through.

Let us read in Mark 4, beginning in verse 35.

It is an event that will help us recognize that even the disciples who walked beside Jesus were subject to the same fragility in faith that we are today:

Mark 4:35-41 NET

35 On that day, when evening came, Jesus said to his disciples, “Let’s go across to the other side of the lake.” 36 So after leaving the crowd, they took him along, just as he was, in the boat, and other boats were with him. 37 Now a great windstorm developed and the waves were breaking into the boat, so that the boat was nearly swamped. 38 But he was in the stern, sleeping on a cushion. They woke him up and said, “Teacher, don’t you care that we are about to die?” 39 So he got up and rebuked the wind, and said to the sea, “Be quiet! Calm down!” Then the wind stopped, and it was dead calm. 40 And he said to them, “Why are you cowardly? Do you still not have faith?” 41 They were overwhelmed by fear and said to one another, “Who then is this? Even the wind and sea obey him!”

WHAT IS FAITH?

Now I return to the question I asked a little while ago:

Is believing in the power of Jesus Christ and knowing Jesus
enough for us to place our complete faith in Him?

Because of the word “completely,” the answer can only be no.

If the question were: Is believing in the power of Jesus Christ and knowing Jesus enough for us to place our faith in Him?

Then the answer would be yes; that is what we do when we surrender our lives to Jesus.

But my desire is not to have a superficial faith;

My desire is to have a deep, complete faith in Jesus;

And I cannot do that by myself.

Therefore, today I will seek to answer this question by exploring our fragility as human beings and demonstrating that, despite our insufficiency, this matter, thank God, does not depend on us or on our ability.

This matter depends on the power of the One who created us;

The One who is above all things;

So, to answer the question, I decided to begin by defining faith.

First I looked in the dictionary; and then, because I like to save the best for last, I looked in the Bible.

Definition of faith in the dictionary:

  1. Absolute belief in the existence or truth of a certain fact;

  2. Absolute trust in something or someone;

  3. Commitment to faithfulness to the word given;

  4. Absolute adherence to what is considered true;

  5. State or attitude of one who believes in or hopes for something.

This definition is already quite complete; but we are more interested in understanding how faith is defined in the Bible.

So, to define faith according to the Word of God, I will highlight 5 characteristics of faith that we find in Scripture.

5 characteristics of faith in Scripture:

  1. Faith is what gives foundation to our hope;

  2. The origin of our faith is in God Himself;

  3. The central object, or focus, of our faith is Jesus Christ;

  4. Faith is fundamental for us to draw near to God;

  5. Faith should result in a life of obedience to God.

1st characteristic

Faith is what gives a firm foundation to our hope.

Hebrews 11:1-2 NET

1 Now faith is being sure of what we hope for, being convinced of what we do not see. 2 For by it the people of old received God’s commendation.

Proverbs 3:5 NET

Trust in the Lord with all your heart, and do not rely on your own understanding.

It is faith that gives foundation to our hope and assures us of what we cannot see, but know to be true.

If our hope were founded on our own understanding, and not on our trust in God, how firm would it be?

It would probably be founded on something like money, political leaders, mystical objects, or anything else that is lost as time passes.

But it is our faith in God, who is eternal and sovereign, that gives foundation to our hope.

This does not mean that it cannot go through struggles, doubts, or fragility; but it means that, whatever moment we are going through, we can always look to Jesus with confidence.

2nd characteristic

The origin of our faith is in God Himself.

Genesis 15:5-6 NET

5 The Lord took him outside and said, “Gaze into the sky and count the stars – if you are able to count them!” Then he said to him, “So will your descendants be.” 6 Abram believed the Lord, and the Lord considered his response of faith as proof of genuine loyalty.

Romans 10:17 NET

Consequently faith comes from what is heard, and what is heard comes through the preached word of Christ.

Since the origin of our faith is in God, then the foundation of our faith is unshakable and absolutely trustworthy.

We can affirm this because we know the character of God.

And it is very important for us to recognize that the origin of faith is in God, because if it were in us, in our trust, or in our conviction, it would depend on our weaknesses, our emotions, and our moods.

Here we see the difference between the act of believing and the object of our belief.

This point reinforces what we already know: it is not God who comes from our faith, but our faith that comes from God.

And thank God that this is so, because no matter how fragile my faith may sometimes be, I have a rock that sustains me.

3rd characteristic

The central object, or focus, of our faith is Jesus Christ.

John 3:16 NET

For this is the way God loved the world: He gave his one and only Son, so that everyone who believes in him will not perish but have eternal life.

John 5:39 NET

You study the scriptures thoroughly because you think in them you possess eternal life, and it is these same scriptures that testify about me,

John 14:6 NET

Jesus replied, “I am the way, and the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father except through me.”

He Himself said it. These verses are the words of the Lord Jesus.

We could read many more passages that show us this, especially because the Scriptures testify about Jesus Christ and point to Him as the agent of our Salvation.

So the central focus of our faith can only be Jesus Christ.

It is in Him that the objective quality of our faith lies, that is, He is the truth in which we place our trust and He is the object of our conviction; He is the one who gives objectivity, or reality, to our act of believing.

4th characteristic

Faith is fundamental for us to draw near to God.

Hebrews 11:6 NET

Now without faith it is impossible to please him, for the one who approaches God must believe that he exists and that he rewards those who seek him.

Romans 5:1-2 NET

1 Therefore, since we have been declared righteous by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ, 2 through whom we have also obtained access by faith into this grace in which we stand, and we rejoice in the hope of God’s glory.

We could not draw near to God if we did not believe that we have been justified by the work of Jesus Christ.

And it is only by having faith in what the Lord Jesus did for us that we can draw near to our God, because, through faith, we have a deep confidence in His mercy.

By faith we know that we have been made a new creation, that we have been adopted as beloved children, and that we have been redeemed by the power of Jesus’ blood.

There is no longer the chasm that existed between us and God, which was maintained by sin, because our Lord Jesus came so that we could be reconciled to God.

It is only through faith in Jesus Christ that we can draw near to God.

5th characteristic

Faith should result in a life of obedience to God.

John 14:21 NET

The person who has my commandments and obeys them is the one who loves me. The one who loves me will be loved by my Father, and I will love him and will reveal myself to him.”

James 2:17 NET

So also faith, if it does not have works, is dead being by itself.

James 2:22 NET

You see that his faith was working together with his works and his faith was perfected by works.

We do not merely believe; but we deeply desire that the declaration of surrendering our lives be transformative.

Our faith is not merely something that we say we have; it is something that we live and that is an integral part of our being and our life.

It is the motivating force behind our attitudes; our works.

That is why James says that faith without works is dead in itself.

None of this is automatic; we will fail because we are human, but because we have true faith, we will always eventually know whose example to follow and whom to draw near to.

We need to know that the natural result of our faith is to live a life of obedience to God.

PUTTING OUR FAITH IN JESUS

So what conclusion can we draw from this?

Placing our faith in Jesus Christ, the Lord of our lives, means believing absolutely in Him, trusting absolutely in Him, and obeying His commandments without reservation.

This is impossible without the power of God, but:

Even with God on our side, faith has to be exercised by us.

Do not think about your faith only when things are easy;

When things are pleasant;

Or when you understand what God is doing in your life.

Exercise your faith in Jesus especially when things are difficult;

When things are unpleasant, when it hurts;

When you do not understand why you are going through this.

Trust in the love and care of the Lord Jesus,

especially when tears fall down your face.

There is a song that says:

And even when I cry, my tears will be

To water my faith and comfort my heart.

Our faith helps us to have a solid foundation so that we may live a life of surrender to what we believe:

That Jesus Christ, the Son of God made man,
came to earth as one of us,
lived a life free from sin, but not free from temptation,
suffered trials: betrayal, pain, abandonment;
but He came out of love,
showed us the truth about God,
the way back to God
and the eternal life that we will have at His side as children,
justified and redeemed by His blood.

We believe these truths and know Jesus, but:

Is that enough for us to place our complete faith in Him?
We know that it was not enough for us, but also
We know that it was not enough for the disciples.

DO YOU STILL NOT HAVE FAITH?

Let us revisit the episode we read at the beginning;

Mark 4, now beginning with verse 37.

Remember, brothers and sisters: by this point the disciples had already witnessed much of Jesus’ power.

So they were with Jesus on the sea, in a boat:

Mark 4:37-41 NET

37 Now a great windstorm developed and the waves were breaking into the boat, so that the boat was nearly swamped. 38 But he was in the stern, sleeping on a cushion. They woke him up and said, “Teacher, don’t you care that we are about to die?” 39 So he got up and rebuked the wind, and said to the sea, “Be quiet! Calm down!” Then the wind stopped, and it was dead calm. 40 And he said to them, “Why are you cowardly? Do you still not have faith?” 41 They were overwhelmed by fear and said to one another, “Who then is this? Even the wind and sea obey him!”

"Do You Still Not Have Faith?"

It was the question Jesus asked the disciples who followed Him, walked alongside Him, learned from Him, ate with Him, and had already witnessed several miracles.

And yet they asked one another:
"Who then is this? Even the wind and sea obey him!"

"Do You Still Not Have Faith?"

How many times has Jesus looked at us and asked us this question?

I would say that this must have happened to me a few times already.

It is not that my faith is not in Jesus; my faith is in Him.

This is something I say, sing, and pray...

But what about when a heavier concern comes?

A frustration, or a problem that I cannot solve?

Someone who has an attitude that is harder to put up with?

A disappointment from a family member, a friend, a brother or sister?

When serious illness comes, or death?

Sometimes, at those times,
I realize how fragile my faith can be.

Not because I stop believing in Jesus or because I stop trusting in Him, but because at those times,

I sometimes forget to get down on my knees
and thank Him for being with me in the boat.

My faith is not great enough to move mountains, nor to command a tree to be transplanted into the sea.

Sometimes my faith is not even enough to keep me from crying in the middle of a storm.

But my faith is enough to trust in something:

In our boat, which is small and fragile, is Jesus Christ.
Our Good Shepherd, our Savior.
And He has authority and power over the wind and the sea.
He has all power, in heaven and on earth.
And He Himself promised that He would never leave us alone.

WAIT FOR GOD ALONE

To conclude, let us return to the question:

Is believing in the power of Jesus Christ and knowing Jesus
enough for us to place our complete faith in Him?

It is not automatic; it is not always easy; and it never depends only on us.

There is always more ground to cover in the pursuit of knowing God.

There is always a need to spend more time in His presence, study His Word more, pray more, and surrender ourselves more fully to Him, so that our faith may grow and be perfected, becoming mature.

This is not so that we will stop going through storms;

Nor so that tears will no longer fall;

But so that in any situation we will remember that Jesus, who is our Lord and our Savior, who is our God, is, and always will be, with us in the boat.

Therefore, whatever you may be going through, always remember that you can cry out to God with confidence, in the powerful name of Jesus Christ, because we know that He has the power to calm any storm.

No matter how difficult a situation may seem to us, nothing is above the power of God.

And everyone who surrenders his or her life to Jesus and acknowledges Him as Lord enters into communion with the Holy Spirit of God;

Who comforts us;

Who teaches us;

Who guides us;

Who empowers us;

And who gives us access to the presence of our Father, who loves us so much.

Let us finish with the Word of God, with two passages.

The first is in Psalm 62, beginning in verse 5.

The subtitle of this Psalm of David has a special connection with today’s message; it says that it is an “Exhortation to trust in God alone”; and both this passage and the one we are about to read next are always excellent foundations for prayer.

Psalm 62:5-8 NET

5 Patiently wait for God alone, my soul! For he is the one who gives me confidence. 6 He alone is my protector and deliverer. He is my refuge; I will not be upended. 7 God delivers me and exalts me; God is my strong protector and my shelter. 8 Trust in him at all times, you people! Pour out your hearts before him! God is our shelter! (Selah)

And now, we conclude with the 2nd letter of Paul to the Corinthians, chapter 4, beginning with verse 6; this is a passage that is very dear to us at home and that we like to remember especially when things become more difficult, because it reinforces an idea that strengthens us:

We may not have, in ourselves, the ability to overcome, or even to endure, a trial.

But we do not need to have that ability, because we have God.

And if God is for us...
